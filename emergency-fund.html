Build Your Emergency Fund

What is an Emergency Fund?

An emergency fund is money saved specifically for unexpected and necessary expenses, such as urgent medical needs, essential repairs, or a temporary loss of income.

It is separate from the money you plan to spend on everyday needs or personal wants.

Why is an Emergency Fund Important?

Prepare for Unexpected Events

Saved money can help you cover urgent costs when an unexpected situation occurs.

Reduce Unnecessary Borrowing

Having savings available may reduce the need to borrow money for certain emergency expenses.

Support Financial Stability

An emergency fund provides a financial buffer that can make difficult situations more manageable.

How to Start an Emergency Fund

Step 1: Identify Essential Expenses

List the basic expenses you need to cover, such as food, housing, transportation, utilities, and required payments.

Step 2: Set a Realistic Goal

Choose a starting savings goal based on your income and responsibilities. Even a small first goal can help you build the habit.

Step 3: Save Regularly

Set aside an amount you can manage whenever you receive an allowance, salary, or other income.

Step 4: Keep the Money Accessible

Choose a suitable place for emergency savings where the money can be accessed when it is genuinely needed.

Step 5: Rebuild After Using It

If you use part of your emergency fund, make a plan to gradually replace the amount when you can.

How Much Should You Save?

One commonly used long-term guideline is to save enough to cover several months of essential expenses, but the right amount depends on your situation.

For example, if essential expenses total ₱8,000 per month, three months would equal ₱24,000 and six months would equal ₱48,000.

Illustrative Emergency Fund Goals
Essential Expenses 3-Month Example 6-Month Example
₱5,000 per month ₱15,000 ₱30,000
₱8,000 per month ₱24,000 ₱48,000
₱10,000 per month ₱30,000 ₱60,000

These amounts are examples only. If you are just starting, focus on a smaller achievable goal before building a larger fund.

Emergency or Non-Emergency?

Possible Emergencies

  • Urgent medical expenses
  • Essential home or vehicle repairs
  • Temporary loss of income
  • Unexpected necessary travel

Usually Not Emergencies

  • Entertainment subscriptions
  • Shopping sales and discounts
  • Optional gadget upgrades
  • Unplanned leisure purchases
