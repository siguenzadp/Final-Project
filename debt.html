Learn Debt Management

What is Debt?

Debt is money borrowed from another person, bank, or financial institution that must be repaid under agreed terms.

Borrowing can help people cover important expenses, but poor debt management may lead to financial difficulties.

Common Types of Debt

Personal Loans

Money borrowed from banks or lending institutions for personal financial needs.

Credit Card Debt

Money owed from purchases made using a credit card. Interest may apply when balances are not paid in full.

Student Loans

Money borrowed to pay for education-related expenses, such as tuition and school fees.

Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL)

A payment arrangement that allows consumers to purchase items and pay in installments. Fees or interest may apply.

Understanding Interest Rates

Interest is the cost of borrowing money. It is usually expressed as a percentage of the amount borrowed.

Example of Simple Interest

Suppose you borrow ₱5,000 with an annual simple interest rate of 10% for one year.

Formula:

Interest = Principal × Rate × Time

Interest = ₱5,000 × 0.10 × 1

Interest = ₱500

Total Repayment = ₱5,500

Note: This is a simplified example. Actual loans may include additional fees, different interest calculations, and repayment schedules.

How to Borrow Responsibly

Borrow Only What You Need

Avoid borrowing more money than necessary.

Understand the Loan Terms

Review the interest rate, payment schedule, penalties, and total repayment amount.

Check Your Ability to Repay

Make sure your budget can accommodate loan payments without sacrificing essential needs.

Pay on Time

Follow repayment deadlines to avoid late payment charges and other consequences.

How to Manage Your Debt

Step 1: List All Your Debts

Record your outstanding balances, interest rates, minimum payments, and due dates.

Step 2: Create a Repayment Budget

Review your income and expenses to determine how much you can allocate toward debt payments.

Step 3: Prioritize Payments

Keep up with required payments and choose a strategy for paying down outstanding balances.

Step 4: Avoid Unnecessary Borrowing

Limit additional borrowing while working on your repayment plan.

Step 5: Monitor Your Progress

Track your remaining balances and adjust your repayment plan when necessary.

Debt Repayment Strategies

Debt Snowball Method

Focus extra payments on your smallest debt balance while maintaining required payments on other debts.

Once the smallest debt is paid off, move to the next smallest balance.

Advantage

Paying off smaller debts can provide motivation and a sense of progress.

Debt Avalanche Method

Focus extra payments on the debt with the highest interest rate while maintaining required payments on other debts.

After paying it off, move to the debt with the next highest interest rate.

Advantage

This method generally reduces total interest costs compared with prioritizing lower-rate debts.

Snowball Repayment Order

  1. Loan A - ₱2,000
  2. Loan B - ₱5,000
  3. Loan C - ₱10,000

Avalanche Repayment Order

  1. Loan C - 15% interest
  2. Loan B - 10% interest
  3. Loan A - 5% interest

These examples illustrate which debt receives extra payments first. Required payments on all debts must still be maintained.

Warning Signs of Debt Problems

Recognizing financial warning signs can help you take action early.

  • Frequently missing payment deadlines
  • Borrowing money to pay existing debts
  • Struggling to cover basic living expenses
  • Relying heavily on credit for everyday purchases
  • Having difficulty tracking outstanding balances

If you are experiencing financial difficulties, consider contacting your lender to discuss available repayment arrangements.
