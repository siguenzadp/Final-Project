Interest Rates Made Simple

Learn how interest works, how it is calculated, and why understanding interest rates is important when managing money.

Learn About Interest

What is an Interest Rate?

An interest rate is a percentage used to calculate the cost of borrowing money or the interest earned on certain savings balances over a specified period.

Interest rates are important because they influence how much borrowers pay and how much savers may earn.

Why is Understanding Interest Important?

Understand Borrowing Costs

Knowing how interest works helps borrowers understand the additional cost of using borrowed money.

Make Informed Financial Decisions

Understanding interest rates helps individuals compare financial products and their payment terms.

Understand Savings Growth

Learning about interest helps savers understand how eligible savings balances may grow over time.

Manage Debt Responsibly

Understanding interest charges can help borrowers plan repayments and avoid unnecessary financial costs.

How Does Interest Work?

Interest may be earned when money is deposited in certain financial accounts or charged when money is borrowed.

Interest on Savings

Some savings accounts pay interest on deposited money according to the financial institution's terms and conditions.

Interest on Loans

Lenders may charge interest when individuals borrow money through loans, credit cards, and other credit products.

Important Interest Rate Terms

Principal

The original amount of money borrowed or deposited before interest is added.

Interest Rate

The percentage used to calculate interest over a specified period.

Loan Term

The period during which a borrower is expected to repay a loan according to its agreement.

Annual Percentage Rate

The APR expresses certain borrowing costs as an annual rate and may include applicable fees in addition to interest, depending on the product and rules.

What is Simple Interest?

Simple interest is calculated using only the original principal amount, the interest rate, and the length of time.

Simple Interest Formula

Simple Interest = Principal × Annual Interest Rate × Time in Years

Sample Calculation

Suppose ₱10,000 earns simple interest at a rate of 5% per year for one year.

  • Principal: ₱10,000
  • Annual Interest Rate: 5%
  • Time: 1 Year
  • Interest Earned: ₱500
  • Total Amount: ₱10,500

The calculation is ₱10,000 × 0.05 × 1 = ₱500. This example assumes no fees, taxes, or additional deposits.

What is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is calculated on the original principal and on interest previously added to the balance.

Compounding can increase savings growth over time, although the final amount depends on the interest rate and how often interest is compounded.

Sample Calculation

Suppose ₱10,000 earns 5% annual interest compounded once per year for two years.

  • Starting Balance: ₱10,000
  • Annual Interest Rate: 5%
  • Time: 2 Years
  • Balance After Year 1: ₱10,500
  • Balance After Year 2: ₱11,025
  • Total Interest Earned: ₱1,025

The second year's interest is calculated on ₱10,500 rather than only the original ₱10,000.

Simple Interest vs. Compound Interest

The following table compares simple interest and annually compounded interest on ₱10,000 at a fixed annual rate of 5%.

The examples are simplified calculations for educational purposes. Actual interest earned or charged may differ because of compounding frequency, changing balances, fees, taxes, or other conditions.

Fixed and Variable Interest Rates

Fixed Interest Rate

A fixed interest rate remains the same during the period specified in a financial agreement.

Variable Interest Rate

A variable interest rate may change over time according to the terms of the financial product.

Tips for Understanding Loan Interest

  • Check whether the quoted interest rate is monthly or annual.
  • Understand how interest is calculated before agreeing to borrow.
  • Compare the total repayment amount rather than only the advertised rate.
  • Review additional fees, penalties, and repayment conditions.
  • Borrow only amounts you can realistically manage and repay.
