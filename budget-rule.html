Understand the 50/30/20 Budgeting Rule

Learn one simple way to organize your income into needs, wants, and savings or extra debt payments.

Explore the Rule

What is the 50/30/20 Rule?

The 50/30/20 rule is a budgeting guideline that divides your take-home income into three categories: 50% for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings and extra debt payments.

It can help beginners understand how to plan their money, but the percentages may need to change depending on personal circumstances.

Breaking Down the 50/30/20 Rule

50% - Needs

Needs are essential expenses such as food, housing, transportation, utilities, and necessary school or work costs.

30% - Wants

Wants are optional expenses such as entertainment, dining out, subscriptions, and non-essential shopping.

20% - Savings and Extra Debt Payments

This category can support savings goals, emergency funds, and additional debt payments beyond required minimum payments.

Sample Monthly Budget

Here is an example of how someone with a take-home income of ₱20,000 per month might use the 50/30/20 guideline.

Example 50/30/20 Monthly Budget
Category Percentage Amount
Needs 50% ₱10,000
Wants 30% ₱6,000
Savings and Extra Debt Payments 20% ₱4,000
Total 100% ₱20,000

This is a sample calculation, not a budget that will fit everyone.

How to Apply the Rule

Step 1: Identify Your Take-Home Income

Start with the amount of money you actually receive after any required deductions.

Step 2: List Your Monthly Expenses

Write down your regular expenses and organize them into needs and wants.

Step 3: Calculate Your Categories

Multiply your income by 0.50, 0.30, and 0.20 to find the suggested amounts for each category.

Step 4: Adjust and Review

Compare the suggested amounts with your real expenses and adjust the plan to reflect your priorities and responsibilities.

What if the Percentages Do Not Fit?

Some people spend more than 50% of their income on essential needs, especially when housing, food, and transportation are expensive.

The 50/30/20 rule is only a starting point. Prioritize essential expenses and required payments, and choose realistic amounts for your savings goals.
